A. Backup architecture — the gateway question

Short answer: your service is a 600 A switchboard feeding the two 200 A panels that carry your backup loads, and the design guide for the quoted equipment sets a 200 A boundary per gateway — with multiple gateways as the manufacturer’s own stated method for services above that. So the quote isn’t a workaround; it’s the documented standard for your exact service — Tesla’s Powerwall 3 System Design Guide, Design Considerations (energylibrary.tesla.com): “Multiple Backup Gateways can be installed on a single site to accommodate sites with greater than 200A service equipment”: one gateway per panel, each panel backed up whole, nothing moved at the switchboard — and the second gateway is in at our cost, no markup. The only route to a single-gateway design that still covers both panels whole is consolidation: reconfigure the switchboard so the whole house lands behind one 200 A boundary, which the code path available here (220.83 — the table covers why 220.87 isn’t) permits only if the full-house calculation comes in under 200 A. That’s a demanding bar for a home your size, and it trades away redundancy to clear it — but it’s your electrician-at-the-switchboard requirement, so our licensed electrician runs that calculation at the engineering site visit, at the board, against your four questions in your words. If it clears and prices under the second gateway, we build it and the price comes down. Entirely your call.

This is why I said a second gateway is what backs up the loads you listed — it’s the by-the-book protocol for your service. If our tech finds a way to cover those same loads without it, he’s instructed to bring you that option and show you how. But the protocol for what you asked for is the second gateway.

The One Big Beautiful Bill Act (H.R. 1, P.L. 119-21, enacted July 4, 2025) already ended the homeowner-side credit (§25D) for purchases after December 31, 2025, and terminates §48E for solar placed in service after December 31, 2027 — the begin-construction exception expired July 4, 2026, so the placed-in-service date is the whole test now.
